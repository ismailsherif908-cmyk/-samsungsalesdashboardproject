# Key Findings & Recommendations

1. **Prioritize the A14 5G and S23 5G.** They already generate the highest
   total revenue of all 15 models ($913M and $895M) — increasing stock
   availability and marketing spend here has the clearest payoff.

2. **Treat regions outside North America as growth targets.** North America
   leads all regions in units sold (2.70M), well ahead of the rest, so the
   other four markets have the most room to grow.

3. **Investigate the 2021–2023 revenue dip before repeating strategy.**
   Revenue peaked in 2020 ($1.96B), fell to a low in 2023 ($1.53B), then
   rebounded in 2024 ($1.92B) — understanding what drove the recovery would
   help sustain it deliberately rather than relying on a natural rebound.

4. **Keep shifting the lineup toward 5G-only models.** 5G-capable phones
   sell about 34% more units on average than non-5G phones (35,667 vs
   26,605 per row), so the older S10–S21 generation looks like the weaker
   long-term bet.

5. **Expand 5G availability and marketing in Latin America.** It has the
   highest average 5G preference of any region (68.7%) but isn't the
   top-selling region — demand there looks under-served.

6. **Don't rely on network-coverage rollout as a sales lever.** Regional 5G
   coverage has almost no correlation with units sold (r = 0.106); pricing,
   brand, and model tier matter more.

7. **Review Q3 pricing and promotions.** Q3 sells the most units of any
   quarter but at the lowest average price, while Q1 generates the highest
   revenue per unit — broad discounting may be trading margin for volume.

8. **Grow S22 5G sales volume, not just its price.** It already has the
   highest average price per unit ($1,237) of any model, showing the market
   will pay a premium — the opportunity is to sell more of it, not raise
   the price further.
